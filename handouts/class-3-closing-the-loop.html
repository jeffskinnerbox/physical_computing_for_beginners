Why your car curves

Equal throttle is not equal speed

No two motors are identical. Brush wear, gearbox friction, and small differences in the windings mean the same duty cycle spins one wheel faster than the other — and equal throttle told both wheels the same thing.

Closed loop — naming the controller

trim += KI × error is the "I" in PID

P — Proportional

React to the error right now

not built

I — Integral

React to the error added up over time

trim += KI * error

← today (Phase 5)

D — Derivative

React to how fast the error is changing

not built — needs a cleaner, faster signal

We stop at I: the sensor reads in about 4 cm/s steps, and the loop runs only four times a second — a D term would amplify that jitter instead of smoothing it. Keep it in your back pocket for a smoother measurement.

Homework — Phase 6

Measure k, then sweep KI and MAX_TRIM

1. measure-k.py

Drive slow, then fast. Compute k = cm/s gained per 1.0 of throttle.

→

2. Starting KI

Suggested KI ≈ 0.3 / k — the middle of the sweep.

→

3. Sweep KI

Try KI × 0.5, 1, 2, 4 with MAX_TRIM loose. Pick the highest calm KI.

→

4. Set MAX_TRIM

≈ 1.5–2× the settled trim, then re-check KI once more.

Change one number at a time. Repeat until two changes in a row improve drift by less than your noise floor.

What you learned

Measuring beats guessing

  • Open-loop control trusts the clock; errors stack move after move.
  • No two motors are identical, so equal throttle is not equal speed.
  • A slot optocoupler's raw signal still bounces — debouncing it (Class 1's fix, reused) turns ticks into a trustworthy wheel speed.
  • Closed-loop control measures, compares, and nudges the faster wheel down — trim += KI × error makes KI an integral controller.
  • Tuning by experiment (Phase 6): score each setting the same way, change one number at a time, stop when gains fall below the noise floor.
  • Matched wheels are not a straight heading — Class 4's IMU closes that gap.
